*My own write-up, August 2024. The figures are from my summary and the company's reporting at the time; check them against the latest filings before you use them.*

Long Auto Partner, held for two to three years. It is an independent distributor of car parts, and in this market the only advantage that compounds is scale: a denser branch and logistics network, more parts on the shelf, faster delivery. Auto Partner grows faster than anyone else and still holds only about **1.7% [E]** of its market, so there is a long way to run before scale stops being a story about taking share.

The business is a logistics platform. It buys spare parts, holds the range, and gets them to the customer quickly. In Poland roughly **62% of its customers are workshops, 30% specialist shops and 8% non-specialist [R]**; exports go entirely to shops, which pay better. About **70% of sales are already online, and 99% of those are international [R]**, so the online channel is where the growth comes from rather than a cost it has to defend.

The demand is anti-cyclical, which is the part I like. When a recession hits, people stop buying new cars, the fleet gets older, and an older fleet needs more repairs, so parts demand goes up rather than down.

I pitched this alongside a short in a larger, slower competitor. Owning the two together strips out the moves that are really about the sector or the Polish market and leaves the bit of the return that is specific to Auto Partner winning share, which is the only bit the thesis is actually about.

The falsifier is share. If quarterly volume growth stops beating the market, the whole "scale is the only edge, and we are winning it" case is wrong, and the multiple no longer deserves the benefit of the doubt.
