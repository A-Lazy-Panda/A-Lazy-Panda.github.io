## Thesis

Auto Partner is an independent aftermarket car-parts distributor. In a market where the only real competitive advantage is scale — density of the branch and logistics network, breadth of SKUs, delivery speed — it is the fastest-growing operator while still holding only an estimated **~1.7% share [E]**. The set-up is a long runway of share gains inside a structurally stable, consolidating category, held for **2–3 years**.

## Why the business works

The model is a logistics platform: buy spare parts, hold breadth and depth of inventory, and deliver fast to the end customer. In Poland roughly **62% of customers are workshops, 30% specialised shops, 8% non-specialised [R]**; exports are 100% to shops, which carry higher margins. About **70% of sales are already online, of which 99% are international [R]** — the online channel is the growth vector, not a defensive necessity.

Demand is **anti-cyclical**: in a downturn new-vehicle sales fall, the car parc ages, and an older fleet needs more maintenance — which lifts parts demand. That is an unusually comfortable place to compound.

## Key figures (as pitched)

- Market cap **zł3.109bn [R]**, share count **130.62m [R]**
- Listings across **WSE / LSE / DB [R]**
- Estimated market share **~1.7% [E]**

## What would break it

Scale advantages erode if a larger pan-European distributor prices to take the Polish market, or if the online channel commoditises delivery so that breadth stops mattering. The falsifier is share: if quarterly volume growth stops outrunning the market, the entire "size is the only edge, and we're winning it" thesis is wrong.
