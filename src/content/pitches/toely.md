*A Cambridge Student Investment Fund pitch, December 2024, which I ran as PM. Buy through the ADR (TOELY) or the less liquid OTC line. Company facts are as stated in the pitch; check them against current filings.*

Long Tokyo Electron, for an IRR of roughly **16.8% [E]**. It is one of the five big makers of wafer-fab equipment, and the only one that builds tools for all four of the key front-end steps. Each of the five owns a different step of the process, and each defends it the same way: by spending more on capex and R&D than a challenger can afford to match.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/toely/s1.png" alt="WFE industry overview and the big five" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>The wafer-fab-equipment industry, and where Tokyo Electron sits among the big five.</figcaption></figure>

The reason the position holds is accumulated know-how, and Tokyo Electron keeps it in the building. Retention runs at about **92% [R]**, and it hires expertise out of competitors rather than losing its own. R&D that works pays for the next generation of tools, which widens the niche again. In its core it holds about **90% of the coater/developer market and 100% of high-NA [R]**.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/toely/s2.png" alt="Competitive advantage cannot be eroded" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>Why the advantage compounds rather than erodes: R&D density feeding the next tool generation.</figcaption></figure>

The timing argument is that the market was treating a cyclical trough as if it were structural. Semiconductors are cyclical, the glut was clearing, and the equipment makers were being priced on depressed forward earnings.

There is also a reason to want the sector at all. Over three, five, ten and fifteen years, semis, semi-cap equipment and EDA have beaten the market, they sit in the middle of everything in tech, and their margins and cash generation have structurally improved. The balance sheet backs the specific name up: **no debt, a steady ~50% payout, and relatively low China exposure [R]** for the industry.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/toely/s6.png" alt="DCF valuation" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>The DCF, on deliberately un-heroic assumptions.</figcaption></figure>

The company has been here before and got it wrong. In the 1980s it under-invested in a downturn, and Korean entrants took share. Management says capex and R&D are set to roughly double over the previous five years to avoid a repeat. That is the thing to watch. The catalysts are the customer build-outs that pull equipment demand through.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/toely/s7.png" alt="Catalyst path" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>The catalyst path: the fab build-outs that pull the order book.</figcaption></figure>

It breaks if that investment lapses, if a process-node shift routes around the niche, or if China risk turns out higher than the low reported number.
