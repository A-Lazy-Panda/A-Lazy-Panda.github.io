*A team pitch, with Flora Fu, Kevin Zeng, Ashwin Bindra, Sze Yu Wang and Emilio Lara. The price target and upside are the team's estimates at the time.*

Long Duolingo to a **'25 target of $176.13, about 85% upside and a 21% IRR [E]**. The core claim is that subscription penetration is being underrated, because the market misreads the core cohort, and it is heading for an inflection rather than a plateau.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/duol/s1.png" alt="Business overview" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>The business: how Duolingo makes money, and where the growth sits.</figcaption></figure>

Start with the product. On the data that matters, Duolingo is the best-in-category learning app, and that is what drives the top line.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/duol/s2.png" alt="Best in category product and market share" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>Best-in-category on the metrics that actually move revenue.</figcaption></figure>

The first leg is the misunderstood customer. The Street reads the cohort as pandemic-era users who churn out; we read it as a younger, stickier base whose paid conversion is only starting.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/duol/s3.png" alt="Misunderstood core customer" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>The core-customer misread: who is actually driving the subscription base.</figcaption></figure>

The second leg is international. Monetisation outside the US and Europe is far behind engagement, which is exactly where a freemium model has its next move.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/duol/s4.png" alt="International monetisation" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>International: two steps backwards on monetisation, one big leap forward to come.</figcaption></figure>

Put those against what the sell-side actually has in its numbers, and the gap is in the penetration curve rather than anything exotic.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/duol/s5.png" alt="Variance to Street" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>Where our numbers sit against consensus, and why.</figcaption></figure>

Value it on a DCF and an exit multiple, cross-check against freemium comps, and the blend gets to the target.

<figure class="figure"><img src="/pitches/img/duol/s6.png" alt="Blended price target" loading="lazy"/><figcaption>The blended price target across methods.</figcaption></figure>

Underneath the segments: subscription is about **72% [R]** of revenue (premium, where the penetration argument lives), advertising **15% [R]** (in-app ads to free users), the Duolingo English Test **10% [R]** (accepted by 18 of the top-20 US undergraduate programmes, and already about a tenth of the market within two years of launch), and other **3% [R]**.

It breaks if paid conversion settles at today's cohort economics, or if the English Test stops taking share from the incumbents.
