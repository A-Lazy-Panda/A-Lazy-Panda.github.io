## Thesis, in brief

Garmin's largest lines are Fitness and Outdoor (together ~two-thirds of group revenue), and Fitness is the swing factor in the equity debate. Consensus embeds roughly **7–8% annual Fitness growth [E]**; my work argued performance wearables can grow closer to **~14% [E]**, with Garmin taking a material share, for two reasons:

1. **Low penetration among younger consumers**, who increasingly treat self-optimisation — "lifemaxing" — as a status signal; and
2. **A hard-to-replicate product lead** in GPS accuracy and battery life, built over decades across endurance sports.

The differentiated part was the evidence: an analysis of **search-trend data** used to test whether category growth and Garmin's share capture were actually accelerating, rather than assuming the niche must mature.

## Why it's interesting as method

It's a clean example of the quantamental approach I like — a qualitative moat argument (accuracy, battery, brand) disciplined by an alternative-data test of the one variable the whole thesis turns on (Fitness volume growth), then reconciled line-by-line against consensus.
