It carries firm branding and uses licensed data (Capital IQ) and Redburn's research templates, so the note, model and deck stay off the public site until I have confirmed I am cleared to share them. What follows is a plain-English description of the approach, in my own words, with no firm materials attached.

Garmin's two biggest lines are Fitness and Outdoor, and together they are about two-thirds of group revenue. Fitness is the one the whole debate turns on. Consensus has it growing at roughly **7-8% a year [E]**; my work argued the number is closer to **14% [E]**, with Garmin taking a large share of it, for two reasons. Younger buyers are under-penetrated and increasingly treat self-optimisation as something worth signalling. And the product lead in GPS accuracy and battery life is built up over years and hard to copy.

The part I actually did the work on was the evidence. Rather than assume a "niche" market has to mature, I used search-trend data to test whether category growth and Garmin's share of it were speeding up or slowing down, and reconciled that line by line against consensus.

It is a clean example of the way I like to work: a qualitative case about accuracy, battery and brand, disciplined by an alternative-data test of the one number the whole thing depends on.
