Consumer choice and the budget set

Consumer choice: choosing the best consumption bundle that can be afforded. (Varian)

Consumption bundle: a list of two numbers, (x1,x2)(x_1,x_2)(x1​,x2​).

  • x1x_1x1​: weight/quantity of good 1.
  • x2x_2x2​: weight/quantity of good 2.

Consider a person with a budget of £4 choosing to buy good A (£1 per kg) vs good B (£2 per kg).

Thus,

cost=x1+2x2,\text{cost}=x_1+2x_2,cost=x1​+2x2​,

yielding the budget line

x1+2x2=4.x_1+2x_2=4.x1​+2x2​=4.

So in general the budget set is defined as the set of bundles such that

B={(x1,x2):p1x1+p2x2≤m, x1,x2≥0}.B=\{(x_1,x_2):p_1x_1+p_2x_2\le m,\ x_1,x_2\ge0\}.B={(x1​,x2​):p1​x1​+p2​x2​≤m, x1​,x2​≥0}.

Assume positive prices and income: p1,p2,m>0p_1,p_2,m>0p1​,p2​,m>0.

We can reduce parameters by picking a numeraire good. The entire budget-line equation can be divided by this numeraire price, p2p_2p2​, to give us

p1p2x1+x2=mp2.\frac{p_1}{p_2}x_1+x_2=\frac{m}{p_2}.p2​p1​​x1​+x2​=p2​m​.

Considering more than two goods, we can consider x1x_1x1​ as the consumption good and x2x_2x2​ as the amount of money set aside for everything else, i.e. x2x_2x2​ is a composite good. If it is measured in money, its price is one.

Changes in price and income

The intercepts are m/p1m/p_1m/p1​ and m/p2m/p_2m/p2​; the slope is −p1/p2-p_1/p_2−p1​/p2​.

A change in the price of good 1 pivots the line around the x2x_2x2​ intercept. A change in income shifts the line in parallel, holding prices fixed. Fixed fees and quantity discounts can give non-linear budget boundaries.

Exercise 1: an unchanged budget line

A budget line is given by p1x1+p2x2=mp_1x_1+p_2x_2=mp1​x1​+p2​x2​=m. The government imposes a lump-sum subsidy of uuu, a quantity tax t1t_1t1​ on good 1, and an ad valorem tax τ2\tau_2τ2​ on good 2.

Find the condition on u,t1,τ2u,t_1,\tau_2u,t1​,τ2​ such that the new budget line coincides with the original budget line.

The new budget line is

(p1+t1)x1+(1+τ2)p2x2=m+u.(p_1+t_1)x_1+(1+\tau_2)p_2x_2=m+u.(p1​+t1​)x1​+(1+τ2​)p2​x2​=m+u.

For it to coincide with the original line, both prices and income must increase in the same proportion:

p1+t1p1=1+τ2=m+um.\frac{p_1+t_1}{p_1}=1+\tau_2=\frac{m+u}{m}.p1​p1​+t1​​=1+τ2​=mm+u​.

So the condition is

t1p1=τ2=um.\boxed{\frac{t_1}{p_1}=\tau_2=\frac{u}{m}}.p1​t1​​=τ2​=mu​​.

The quantity tax and lump-sum subsidy are amounts; the ad valorem tax is a rate. They are not generally equal to one another.

Exercise 2: entrance fees at two shops

Suppose Bob has £100 that he can spend to buy sweets from shops A and B. Shop A charges an entrance fee of £10 and sells chocolate candies and ice cream at prices of £5 and £10 per kilogram, respectively. Shop B charges an entrance fee of £20 and sells identical chocolate candies and ice cream at prices of £10 and £5 per kilogram.

Plotting the weight of chocolate candies on the horizontal axis and weight of ice cream on the vertical axis, draw Bob’s budget set.

If Bob goes to shop A, he is left with £90 to spend, and the associated budget line is 5x+10y=905x+10y=905x+10y=90, that is, x+2y=18x+2y=18x+2y=18.

If Bob goes to shop B, he is left with £80 to spend, and the associated budget line is 10x+5y=8010x+5y=8010x+5y=80, that is, 2x+y=162x+y=162x+y=16.

We should also consider the line corresponding to shopping at both shops. Bob pays £30 in entrance fees, then buys chocolate at A and ice cream at B, each for £5 per kilogram. This gives 5x+5y=705x+5y=705x+5y=70, or x+y=14x+y=14x+y=14.

The overall budget frontier is the maximum of the three affordable options:

y={16−2x,0≤x≤2,14−x,2≤x≤10,9−x2,10≤x≤18.y=\begin{cases} 16-2x,&0\le x\le2,\\ 14-x,&2\le x\le10,\\ 9-\frac{x}{2},&10\le x\le18. \end{cases}y=⎩⎨⎧​16−2x,14−x,9−2x​,​0≤x≤2,2≤x≤10,10≤x≤18.​

There are three segments and two interior kinks, at (2,12)(2,12)(2,12) and (10,4)(10,4)(10,4). The budget set is not convex: the midpoint (9,8)(9,8)(9,8) of the affordable intercepts (0,16)(0,16)(0,16) and (18,0)(18,0)(18,0) is unaffordable.
