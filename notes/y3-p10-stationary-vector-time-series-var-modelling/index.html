Lecture Notes

Moving Away from Structural Models

Before VARs, macroeconomists used huge systems of equations with strict Exclusion Restrictions (deciding certain variables didn’t affect others). Two major papers destroyed this approach:

The Lucas Critique (1976)

  • The Idea: People change their behavior when government policies change.
  • The Problem: If you estimate a model based on old policy data, those “slopes” (parameters) will disappear the moment you change the rules. Your model becomes a “useless” map of a country that no longer exists.
  • The Solution: Focus on “deep structural parameters” (like how much people value future money) which don’t change with policy. Micro-foundations not massive model.

The Sims Critique (1980) → VAR relevant

  • The Idea: Most theory-based restrictions are “incredible” (untested and arbitrary).
  • The Problem: Why assume Interest Rates don’t affect GDP today? Theory might say so, but “Reality” (the title of his paper) often says otherwise.
  • The Solution: Treat everything as endogenous. Let every variable influence every other variable. This is the VAR approach.

Defining a VAR Model

Why it’s called “Vector Autoregression”

  • Regression: Predicting a variable using other variables.
  • Auto (Self): Predicting a variable using its own past.
  • Vector: Doing it for a whole group of variables at once.

What is the regressor xtx_txt​?

In a VAR, we don’t just have one dependent variable; we have a “vector” of variables. If we are studying the relationship between Money Growth (Δmt\Delta m_tΔmt​) and Output Growth (Δyt\Delta y_tΔyt​), we package them into a single object:

xt=[ΔmtΔyt]x_t = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta m_t \\ \Delta y_t \end{bmatrix}xt​=[Δmt​Δyt​​]

Instead of one equation, we are now solving for the movement of this entire block of data over time.

Reduced Form

The Structural Form is impossible to estimate directly because if you look at it there is a simultaneity problem, e.g. look at multiplying out A0xtA_0 x_tA0​xt​:

[1a120a2101]⏟A0[x1tx2t]⏟xt=[x1t+a120x2ta210x1t+1x2t]\underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & a_{12}^0 \\ a_{21}^0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} }_{A_0} \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} x_{1t} \\ x_{2t} \end{bmatrix} }_{x_t} = \begin{bmatrix} x_{1t} + a_{12}^0 x_{2t} \\ a_{21}^0 x_{1t} + 1x_{2t} \end{bmatrix}A0​[1a210​​a120​1​]​​xt​[x1t​x2t​​]​​=[x1t​+a120​x2t​a210​x1t​+1x2t​​]

If you put this back into the full structural equation (A0xt=A1xt−1+εtA_0 x_t = A_1 x_{t-1} + \varepsilon_tA0​xt​=A1​xt−1​+εt​), Equation 1 looks like this:

x1t+a120x2t=(lags)+ε1tx_{1t} + \mathbf{a_{12}^0 x_{2t}} = (\text{lags}) + \varepsilon_{1t}x1t​+a120​x2t​=(lags)+ε1t​

To fix this, we multiply the entire system by the inverse, A0−1A_0^{-1}A0−1​ so today’s variables (xtx_txt​) are all alone on the left-hand side, and we don’t have both x1t,x2tx_{1t}, x_{2t}x1t​,x2t​ on LHS:

xt=A0−1A1⏟C1xt−1+A0−1A2⏟C2xt−2+⋯+A0−1Ap⏟Cpxt−p+A0−1εt⏟etx_t = \underbrace{A_0^{-1}A_1}_{C_1} x_{t-1} + \underbrace{A_0^{-1}A_2}_{C_2} x_{t-2} + \dots + \underbrace{A_0^{-1}A_p}_{C_p} x_{t-p} + \underbrace{A_0^{-1}\varepsilon_t}_{e_t}xt​=C1​A0−1​A1​​​xt−1​+C2​A0−1​A2​​​xt−2​+⋯+Cp​A0−1​Ap​​​xt−p​+et​A0−1​εt​​​
  • That is in vector form, you can see each individual equation is also reduced form

We write this as C’s and this is now the VAR(p) model:

xt=C1xt−1+C2xt−2+⋯+Cpxt−p+etx_t = C_1 x_{t-1} + C_2 x_{t-2} + \dots + C_p x_{t-p} + e_txt​=C1​xt−1​+C2​xt−2​+⋯+Cp​xt−p​+et​
  • The Coefficients (C1…CpC_1 \dots C_pC1​…Cp​): These are the Reduced Form parameters. We can estimate these perfectly using OLS (equation-by-equation).
  • The Residuals (ete_tet​): These are “Reduced Form” errors. They are not pure; they are a mathematical mix of the original structural shocks εt\varepsilon_tεt​.

Multivariate Wold Decomposition (VMA → VAR)

The VAR model isn’t just a convenient guess; it is mathematically grounded in Wold’s Decomposition Theorem.

Recall from Wold’s decomposition that any stationary vector of variables, xtx_txt​, can be represented as a infinite sum of past structural shocks:

xt=Θ(L)εt=∑h=0∞θhεt−hx_t = \Theta(L)\varepsilon_t = \sum_{h=0}^{\infty} \theta_h \varepsilon_{t-h}xt​=Θ(L)εt​=h=0∑∞​θh​εt−h​
  • This is a VMA, a vector moving average representation
  • Θ(L)\Theta(L)Θ(L) is a matrix polynomial in the lag operator LLL Θ(L)=θ0+θ1L1+θ2L2+⋯+θhLh\Theta(L) = \theta_0 + \theta_1 L^1 + \theta_2 L^2 + \dots + \theta_h L^hΘ(L)=θ0​+θ1​L1+θ2​L2+⋯+θh​Lh
  • But these θi\theta_iθi​, these are actually matrices, they are n x n matrices

So in our 2 equation case, the Wold representation of the vector xtx_txt​ looks like this: [ΔmtΔyt]=[θ110θ120θ210θ220]⏟θ0[ε1tε2t]+[θ111θ121θ211θ221]⏟θ1[ε1,t−1ε2,t−1]+… \begin{bmatrix} \Delta m_t \\ \Delta y_t \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \theta_{11}^0 & \theta_{12}^0 \\ \theta_{21}^0 & \theta_{22}^0 \end{bmatrix}}_{\theta_0} \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{1t} \\ \varepsilon_{2t} \end{bmatrix} + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \theta_{11}^1 & \theta_{12}^1 \\ \theta_{21}^1 & \theta_{22}^1 \end{bmatrix}}_{\theta_1} \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{1,t-1} \\ \varepsilon_{2,t-1} \end{bmatrix} + \dots [Δmt​Δyt​​]=θ0​[θ110​θ210​​θ120​θ220​​]​​[ε1t​ε2t​​]+θ1​[θ111​θ211​​θ121​θ221​​]​​[ε1,t−1​ε2,t−1​​]+… We can’t estimate the VMA because shocks are invisible. However, if the system is invertible, we can write Θ(L)\Theta(L)Θ(L) as a ratio of two polynomials, a(L)−1b(L)a(L)^{-1}b(L)a(L)−1b(L), and get a VARMA representation instead:

xt=a(L)−1b(L)ϵt  ⟹  a(L)xt=b(L)εtx_t = a(L)^{-1}b(L) \epsilon_t \\ \implies a(L)x_t = b(L)\varepsilon_txt​=a(L)−1b(L)ϵt​⟹a(L)xt​=b(L)εt​

If b(L) = 1, then we remove the MA component so

a(L)xt=εta(L) x_t = \varepsilon_ta(L)xt​=εt​

This is the pure VAR model we found from before. And this is what makes us happy to use ‘past data’.

  • As long as vector xtx_txt​ is stationary
  • Then it has a pure VMA representation
  • Which also has a pure VAR representation
    • Which means past observations can be used to represent hidden past shocks

Identification

What do we know?

Now that we know a VAR is a valid way to represent the economy, we face the problem that the reduced form alone does not fully identify the structural parameter the A′sA'sA′s

xt=C1xt−1+C2xt−2+⋯+Cpxt−p+etx_t = C_1 x_{t-1} + C_2 x_{t-2} + \dots + C_p x_{t-p} + e_txt​=C1​xt−1​+C2​xt−2​+⋯+Cp​xt−p​+et​

Sure, we can recover estimates C^1,C^2,...,C^p\hat C_1, \hat C_2,..., \hat C_pC^1​,C^2​,...,C^p​ and the covariance matrix Σ^\hat \SigmaΣ^,

  • And, to be honest that is good enough if we only want to do forecasting, and do not actually care about the underlying parameters what they are.
  • Once the variables to be included have been chosen VAR model fitting really involves only one parameter: the model order p.
    • For that, use information criteria evaluated over a range of model orders.

But we are often also interested in the true structural model, because this tells us something about the structure of the economy.

To do that we need to check if we can actually identify the structural model.

How many restrictions do we need for identification?

The “Knowns” (Reduced Form)

  • The ′C′'C'′C′ matrices: There are ppp of these matrices, each sized n×nn \times nn×n.

    • That is pn2pn^2pn2 parameters.

  • The Covariance Matrix Σ\SigmaΣ: This is the n x n error matrix of e, not structural error matrix

    • Because it’s symmetric, it has n(n+1)2\frac{n(n+1)}{2}2n(n+1)​ unique parameters.

  • Total known parameters = [pn2+n(n+1)/2][pn^2 + n(n+1)/2][pn2+n(n+1)/2]

The “Unknowns” (Structural Form)

  • The AAA matrices (A0,A1,…,ApA_0, A_1, \dots, A_pA0​,A1​,…,Ap​): There are p+1p+1p+1 matrices (including A0A_0A0​).
    • That is (p+1)n2(p+1)n^2(p+1)n2 parameters.
  • The Structural Covariance Σε\Sigma_\varepsilonΣε​: This is the n x n structural shock matrix,
    • Symmetric, so it also has n(n+1)/2n(n+1)/2n(n+1)/2 parameters.
  • Total Unknowns: [(p+1)n2+n(n+1)/2][(p+1)n^2 + n(n+1)/2][(p+1)n2+n(n+1)/2]

The “Gap” So the gap is drive entirely by the size of the A0A_0A0​ matrix, see this by subtraction:

[(p+1)n2+… ]−[pn2+… ]=n2[(p+1)n^2 + \dots] - [pn^2 + \dots] = \mathbf{n^2}[(p+1)n2+…]−[pn2+…]=n2

This is why we need exactly n2n^2n2 restrictions to identify the model.

What restrictions do we make? (3 types)

Type 1: Normalisation (nnn restrictions) Recall, we set the diagonal of A0A_0A0​ to 1, that diagonal we no longer have to estimate it.

[1a120a2101]⏟A0\underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & a_{12}^0 \\ a_{21}^0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} }_{A_0}A0​[1a210​​a120​1​]​​
  • This just lets us say “Equation 1 is the equation for x1tx_{1t}x1t​.”
  • We need a further n(n−1)n(n-1)n(n−1) restrictions

Type 2: Assume orthogonal shocks We assume that structural shocks (like a “Technology shock” vs a “Monetary policy shock”) are completely uncorrelated. The assumption that the structural shocks are orthogonal can be written as:

E(εε′)=I  ⟹  Σ=A0−1(A0−1)′E(\varepsilon\varepsilon') = I \implies \Sigma = A_0^{-1}(A_0^{-1})'E(εε′)=I⟹Σ=A0−1​(A0−1​)′
  • This forces the off-diagonal elements of Σε\Sigma_\varepsilonΣε​ to be Zero.
  • There are n(n−1)/2n(n-1)/2n(n−1)/2, off diagonal spots in the matrix.
  • We need a further n(n+1)/2n(n+1)/2n(n+1)/2 restrictions

This is often thought a natural assumption since it follows from the modelling strategy in which unobserved structural shocks are viewed as distinct phenomena

Interaction still happens onto other variables, just not error to error - Instead, only through what is embodied in the economic model, as represented by the lag structure. - i.e. In our 2x2 case, any contemporaneous cross equation impacts arise through non-zero values of a12 and a21, not direct from error to error.

Type 3B: Or use long run restrictions

3A: Recursive Restriction - Sims

This is the default restriction. It is a “Short-Run” strategy as it makes assumptions about what happens instantly (within the same time period ttt)

The Core Concept: Waterfall Effect
In a standard VAR, everything affects everything. Recursive identification puts a stop to that.

We assume the economy works like a waterfall:

  • Variable 1 hits everything below it instantly
    • There is nothing before itself that influences it; it is the leader.
  • Variable 2 hits everything below it instantly,
    • Only hits Variable 1 (which is above), with a lag (tomorrow).
  • Variable nnn hits nothing instantly;
    • it only reacts to the others and waits until tomorrow to influence them.

Mathematically what does this mean? It means the A0A_0A0​ matrix is now Lower Triangular. This means every cell above the diagonal is set to zero.

Consider a a 3-variable system: (x1=Bank Rate,x2=Inflation,x3=GDPx_1 = \text{Bank Rate}, x_2 = \text{Inflation}, x_3 = \text{GDP}x1​=Bank Rate,x2​=Inflation,x3​=GDP), we apply recursive restriction to make its A0A_0A0​ matrix lower triangular

[100a21010a310a3201]⏟A0[x1tx2tx3t]=lags+[ε1tε2tε3t]\underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ a_{21}^0 & 1 & 0 \\ a_{31}^0 & a_{32}^0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} }_{A_0} \begin{bmatrix} x_{1t} \\ x_{2t} \\ x_{3t} \end{bmatrix} = \text{lags}+ \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{1t} \\ \varepsilon_{2t} \\ \varepsilon_{3t} \end{bmatrix}A0​​1a210​a310​​01a320​​001​​​​​x1t​x2t​x3t​​​=lags+​ε1t​ε2t​ε3t​​​
  • Row 1 (The Leader): 1x1t=(lags)+ε1t1x_{1t} = (\text{lags}) + \varepsilon_{1t}1x1t​=(lags)+ε1t​. Notice there is no x2x_2x2​ or x3x_3x3​ here. Interest rates are determined only by their own history and a pure shock.
  • Row 2: a210x1t+1x2t=…a_{21}^0 x_{1t} + 1x_{2t} = \dotsa210​x1t​+1x2t​=… Inflation (x2x_2x2​) can be hit by Interest Rates (x1x_1x1​) instantly.
  • Row 3 (The Follower): a310x1t+a320x2t+1x3t=…a_{31}^0 x_{1t} + a_{32}^0 x_{2t} + 1x_{3t} = \dotsa310​x1t​+a320​x2t​+1x3t​=…. GDP (x3x_3x3​) is hit by both x1x_1x1​ and x2x_2x2​ instantly.

Sims argued that many economic interactions have a natural “order” due to information lags or stickiness. This is typical chains of reasoning.


This perfectly closes the identification gap, because the A0A_0A0​ matrix is n x n, so by setting all upper traiangle cells to 0 we get exactly n(n-1)/2 restrictions.

Sensitivity Problem There is a big catch with Option 3A: The results depend on the order you choose. If you put GDP first and Interest Rates last, you are assuming GDP hits Interest Rates instantly, but Interest Rates don’t hit GDP until next month. This will produce different Impulse Response Functions than if you flipped the order.

Key Exam Tip: When using a Cholesky/Recursive identification, you must justify the ordering of your variables based on economic theory (e.g., “Variables that are more ‘sluggish’ should be placed later in the vector”, “The most exogenous variable in SR is placed first in the vector”).

3B: Long-run Restriction - Blanchard and Quah (1989)

Instead of forcing A0A_0A0​ (the instant effects) to be lower triangular, Blanchard and Quah force the Long-Run Impact Matrix to be lower triangular.

They distinguish between two types of shocks based on their persistence.

  • Supply-side (Real): Have a permanent effect on the level of variables like GDP (e.g., a new technology makes the economy more productive forever).
  • Demand-side (Nominal): Have a transitory effect. They might move GDP today, but eventually, the effect dies out, and GDP returns to its “natural” level.

So, let’s show this for a 2 variable economy. Phillips economy where we have that Output Growth (Δyt\Delta y_tΔyt​) and Unemployment (utu_tut​)

xt=[Δytut]\bold x_t = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta y_{t} \\ u_{t} \end{bmatrix}xt​=[Δyt​ut​​]
  • In their formulation both structural shocks will have a zero long run effect on unemployment and the growth of output, this is because they are stationary variables so can’t have growth in LR.
  • But level of output can be higher in the LR

Recall from the Wold Decomposition that xt=Θ(L)εt=[θ1+…θh]εtx_t = \Theta(L)\varepsilon_t = [\theta_1 + \dots \theta_h] \varepsilon_txt​=Θ(L)εt​=[θ1​+…θh​]εt​.

And recall the long-run impact of a shock is the sum of all its effects over time:

Long Run Effect=∑h=0∞θh=Θˉ\text{Long Run Effect} = \sum_{h=0}^{\infty} \theta_h = \bar{\Theta}Long Run Effect=h=0∑∞​θh​=Θˉ

This matrix, Θˉ\bar{\Theta}Θˉ, is the LR cumulative impact matrix. We set its upper triangle to zero. So in our Phillips economy,

[ΔyLRuLR]=[θˉ110θˉ21θˉ22][εSupplyεDemand]\begin{bmatrix} \Delta y_{LR} \\ u_{LR} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \bar{\theta}_{11} & \mathbf{0} \\ \bar{\theta}_{21} & \bar{\theta}_{22} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{Supply} \\ \varepsilon_{Demand} \end{bmatrix}[ΔyLR​uLR​​]=[θˉ11​θˉ21​​0θˉ22​​][εSupply​εDemand​​]
  • The Zero (θˉ12=0\bar{\theta}_{12} = 0θˉ12​=0): This represents the restriction. It says Demand Shock (εDemand\varepsilon_{Demand}εDemand​) has zero total effect on the level of output in the long run.
  • The Supply Shock is allowed to change the level of output permanently.

Just like in Option A, this “Zero” provides the final n(n−1)/2n(n-1)/2n(n−1)/2 restriction needed to solve the system.

  • In a 2-variable system, you need that extra 1 restriction.
  • By deciding that one shock is “neutral” in the long run, you provide that 1 restriction.
  • The math then works backward from the long run to figure out what the short-run A0A_0A0​ matrix must have looked like to make that long-run result possible.

Note that this relies on the Wold theorem to try and do the difficult task of figuring out what the original representation looks like, though we know it is theoretically possible.

Pros

  • Theory-Driven: It doesn’t matter which variable you “list first” in your software. What matters is which shock you restrict to be neutral.
  • Short-run freedom: No need for any ordering in the first place

Cons

  • Stationarity Requirement: For this to work, the variables in xtx_txt​ must be stationary (like growth rates Δyt\Delta y_tΔyt​). If you used the level of GDP, it wouldn’t return to a mean, and the “sum of impacts” wouldn’t be well-defined.
  • This gets really hard to justify beyond this real and nominal framework

Impulse Response Functions

Definition

An impulse response function measures the time profile of the effect of shocks at a give point in time on the values of the variables in a dynamical system in the future.

An IRF is a conceptual experiment. It answers the question:

“If a specific structural shock (ε\varepsilonε) hits the economy today, how will all the variables in the vector xxx evolve over the next hhh periods?”

We write this, as the impulse response of a shock: IyI_yIy​

Iy(h,δ,Ωt−10)=E(yt+h∣Shock hits at t)⏟Scenario A−E(yt+h∣No shock hits at t)⏟Scenario BI_y(h, \delta, \Omega^0_{t-1}) = \underbrace{E(y_{t+h} | \text{Shock hits at } t)}_{\text{Scenario A}} - \underbrace{E(y_{t+h} | \text{No shock hits at } t)}_{\text{Scenario B}}Iy​(h,δ,Ωt−10​)=Scenario AE(yt+h​∣Shock hits at t)​​−Scenario BE(yt+h​∣No shock hits at t)​​
  • assume no other shocks in the system between t and t+h
  • impulse response shows the isolated “ripple effect” of that one shock in h periods.
